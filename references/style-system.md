# Visual system

## Fixed identity

- Use a vertical 3:5 canvas by default.
- Make a low-saturation fibrous paper field the largest area. Choose its hue and value from `background-contrast.md`; do not default to cream or gray-beige.
- Leave roughly 45%–70% coherent negative space. Do not count pale full-page detail as empty space.
- Center the story on one recognizable dog or one clearly related pet group and one calm emotional beat.
- Construct the dog with selective relief-cut drawing: exposed-paper cuts, directional hatching, broken contours, compact cross-cuts, and a few flat ink fields.
- Use two to four inks. Include one decisive chromatic dark and zero to two brighter accents.
- Keep typography tiny and subordinate. The image must work without readable text.
- Preserve handmade defects: uneven pressure, slight misregistration, dry-brush gaps, paper fibers, and imperfect edges.

## Dog construction

- Establish the silhouette first: head-to-body ratio, muzzle, ears, legs, chest, tail, and pose.
- Preserve breed or individual identity through three to six anchors rather than exhaustive fur detail.
- Let carved direction explain form: short arcs around the muzzle and cheeks, strokes following the chest and shoulder planes, longer broken cuts down the torso, compact marks at joints and shadow turns.
- Use three mark-density zones: open paper, medium directional hatching, and localized dense cuts. Never cover the entire dog with uniform scratches.
- Keep eyes, nose, mouth, and paws simple but correctly placed. Avoid human-like facial expressions and malformed limbs.
- Abstract the background more strongly than the dog. Let outer contours dissolve into paper.

## Composition and hierarchy

- Let the dog or dog-centered cluster occupy about 20%–42% of the page. For multiple pets, preserve the source count, relative size, and enough paper gaps to keep silhouettes distinct.
- Prefer one dominant silhouette, one supporting environmental rhythm, and optional microtype.
- When a photo supplies useful setting cues, keep one primary environment anchor plus up to two micro-accents within 8%–15% of the canvas (18% maximum). Preserve at least 55%–65% coherent negative space and let environmental forms dissolve into paper.
- For the confirmed fused-companion hierarchy, place the dog slightly left of center in the lower third at 34%–38% page height, reserve 60%–68% coherent blank paper, and place a single low-contrast object whisper at the lower-right. Read `reference-fusion.md` for exact role and placement locks.
- Keep dense dark ink below roughly 20%–28% of the canvas, but print it with confident saturation.
- Use stable spacing, gentle asymmetry, and a clear resting area. Avoid urgent diagonals, visual explosions, and competing focal points.
- Allow a crop only when it improves intimacy; do not accidentally cut paws, ears, or tail at awkward joints.

## Color behavior

- Analyze the dog's dominant coat before fixing the paper. Record a paper contrast lock and keep it through generation and retries.
- Use chromatic darks such as forest green, mineral blue, aubergine, rust, or blue-gray instead of dominant black.
- Use accents as quiet fields or small balanced blocks, never as neon alarms.
- Form transitions with separated blocks, overprint, broken hatching, and exposed paper—not smooth digital gradients.
- Keep paper softly light and matte, with enough hue or value separation to reveal the dog's silhouette. Avoid pure white, muddy brown, sepia wash, faux leather, and heavy archival aging.

## Emotional range

- **Tender:** warm cream, rust, dusty coral; close pose; soft broken contours.
- **Playful:** cobalt or green with lemon/tangerine; buoyant spacing; small repeated marks without speed effects.
- **Solitary:** mineral blue, violet-blue, cool white; distant dog; large quiet field.
- **Sleepy:** warm gray, apricot, deep green; curled silhouette; low horizontal rhythm.
- **Rainy:** blue-gray, cyan, isolated red or yellow; sparse vertical cuts and open paper gaps.
- **Outdoors:** fresh green, sky blue, persimmon; simplified grass, ridge, wind, or water bands.

## Avoid

- generic vector mascot, kawaii sticker sheet, emoji face, Disney-like character, children's clip art;
- photorealistic fur with a wood-grain overlay;
- full-page black linocut, Victorian engraving, antique book plate, rustic kraft collage;
- thick uniform outline, glossy gradients, 3D render, cinematic lens effects, product mockup;
- random decorative bones, paw icons, hearts, stars, or text not requested;
- copied reference text, brand marks, signatures, watermarks, or exact layouts.
