# Adaptive paper contrast

Choose paper after inspecting the dog. Treat negative space as a colored compositional field, not a fixed cream background.

## Build the paper contrast lock

1. Identify the dominant visible coat family: warm brown/apricot, golden/yellow, red/rust, white/cream, black/charcoal, cool gray/blue, or mixed.
2. Estimate the coat's broad value: light, middle, or dark. Ignore tiny eyes, nose, tags, and garment stripes.
3. Choose a low-saturation paper that differs in temperature or value while remaining calm and printable.
4. Record: `PAPER CONTRAST LOCK — coat: [family + value]; paper: [hue + value]; separation: [temperature/value strategy].`
5. Keep that lock through prompt compilation, generation, and retries.

## Routing table

- **Warm brown, caramel, apricot, tan:** use mist blue-gray, pale mineral blue, cool sage-gray, or very soft lavender-gray. Never default to gray-beige, oat, cream, tan, kraft, or warm ivory.
- **Golden, yellow, honey:** use pale lavender-gray, cool blue-gray, or muted mineral blue. Avoid yellow cream and warm beige.
- **Red, copper, rust:** use desaturated sage, cool green-gray, or pale blue-green. Avoid peach, terracotta, rust paper, and warm pink-beige.
- **White, cream, very pale:** use a light-middle dusty blue, sage-gray, lavender-gray, or neutral cool gray. Avoid pure white and near-white cream.
- **Black, charcoal, very dark:** use light oat, pale peach-gray, soft warm gray, or subdued cool paper. Keep the paper light enough for value separation but not glowing white.
- **Cool gray, blue-gray:** use pale oat, soft peach-gray, muted warm ivory, or dusty apricot-gray. Avoid similar blue-gray paper.
- **Mixed or patterned:** choose against the largest coat region. If two regions are equal, prefer a neutral paper that separates by value from both; do not match the most colorful accessory.

## Value and saturation rules

- Keep paper saturation low so it reads as material rather than a flat colored backdrop.
- For a light coat, choose a light-middle paper; for a dark coat, choose a light paper; for a middle-value coat, create temperature contrast and a modest value step.
- Do not use pure white. Preserve visible paper fibers and a stable midtone.
- Do not make both paper and coat simultaneously pale, warm, and low-contrast.
- Let white markings retain pale colored cuts so they remain visible against the adaptive paper.

## Thumbnail correction order

If the subject disappears at thumbnail size, correct in this order:

1. shift paper temperature away from the coat;
2. adjust paper value one step lighter or darker;
3. strengthen internal color blocks slightly;
4. deepen only tiny structural accents.

Never solve a paper/coat collision by adding a continuous dark outline, dense hatch halo, drop shadow, sticker border, or cutout shape.

## Examples

- Apricot curly dog → cool mist blue-gray paper + rust/apricot coat inks + muted slate structural marks.
- White fluffy dog → dusty sage-gray paper + warm white exposed cuts + mineral-blue structure.
- Black dog → pale oat paper + charcoal/indigo ink + one restrained warm accent.
- Blue-gray dog → soft peach-gray paper + mineral blue ink + muted rust accent.
